# ADR Reporting & Documentation

## Task 2 — Structured ADR Reporting Project

### Project Type
**Theoretical / simulated pharmacovigilance case study**

This project demonstrates how a suspected adverse drug reaction (ADR) can be documented using a structured ADR reporting approach. The patient case below is fictional and is created for educational purposes because the internship task provides a theoretical workflow rather than a supplied patient case.

---

# 1. Fictional Patient Case

A 45-year-old female patient was prescribed **amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily** for a presumed bacterial respiratory infection.

Amoxicillin was started on **1 September 2026**. On **3 September 2026**, approximately two days after treatment began, the patient developed a generalized skin rash and itching accompanied by nausea and dizziness.

The patient contacted a healthcare professional. Amoxicillin was discontinued. The rash and itching gradually improved over the following days, and the patient recovered without hospitalization or permanent impairment.

For this educational example, paracetamol was reported as an occasional concomitant medicine.

# 3. Causality Consideration

The reaction has a **plausible temporal relationship** with amoxicillin because treatment began before the reported symptoms appeared.

The improvement after discontinuation also provides supportive information (dechallenge). However, this educational case does not contain enough clinical information to establish definite causality.

A real pharmacovigilance assessment would consider additional information such as:

- patient's medical history
- indication for treatment
- other medicines and exposures
- previous allergy history
- clinical examination
- laboratory findings where relevant
- exact dechallenge course
- rechallenge information, if any
- alternative explanations

Therefore, the appropriate educational conclusion is:

**Amoxicillin is the suspected medicine associated with the reported reaction, with a possible drug-related ADR based on the available chronology.**

# 4. ADR Reporting Workflow Demonstrated

```text
Patient develops symptoms
        ↓
Review medication history
        ↓
Identify suspected medicine
        ↓
Establish chronology
        ↓
Document reaction and dates
        ↓
Record outcome and seriousness
        ↓
Document reporter information
        ↓
Add relevant clinical information
        ↓
Submit/report through the appropriate pharmacovigilance system
```

---

# 5. Key Pharmacovigilance Principles

### Completeness
A useful ADR report should contain enough information to identify the patient, suspected medicine, adverse event and reporter.

### Accuracy
Dates, doses, routes, symptoms and outcomes should be documented as accurately as possible.

### Traceability
The suspected medicine and timeline should be clearly documented so that the case can be reviewed.

### Confidentiality
Personally identifiable patient information should not be unnecessarily exposed in educational or public repositories.

### Follow-up
Additional information may be collected when needed to clarify the case or assess causality.
